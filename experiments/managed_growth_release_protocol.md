# When should the trainer release the fourth group?

Prospective follow-up to the [managed growth pilot](managed_growth_protocol.md), 25 November 2026. Primary question: can the external trainer reduce B's return cost relative to leaving growth enabled while preserving A, C and D? Releasing permission sets the active local group budget back to three. The existing review chooses which groups to pool. Fast/slow evidence remains available at fine addresses. There is one opportunity to grant growth and at most one release per path.

Use 24 new paired seeds (4200–4223). Reuse the valuable C/D world and opening policy. The three histories share everything through tick 640. B then returns to .9 permanently, stays at .1 throughout, or returns to .9 for 128 ticks then goes back to .1. C/D stay .8/.2. End at tick 1024. Use common random inputs and matched binary outcomes where rates agree, including the independent review batches. All paths, including initial refusals, count.

Fork just before review 384. All arms use the original opening inspector. Compare keeping its permission throughout, scheduling release 384 ticks after the decision (tick 768), and checking the existing fast-evidence inspector every 64 ticks from 448. In the monitored arm only, two consecutive predicted fourth-group gains at or below the original .01 opening threshold withdraw permission before that review. No regime, future observations or review labels enter the decision. No re-opening or forced choice of merger. The timer is a comparator, not an oracle.

Score pre-label Brier separately for A, B, C and D and equally over the two tasks. Report windows 385–640, 641–832, 833–1024, 641–1024, and 769–1024 after the short return ends. Record command times and the actual local partitions per review. The primary criterion is mean B gain on lasting return above .005 over 641–1024 with a positive lower paired 95% seed-bootstrap bound against keeping permission. Require mean cost below .01 on each other source in that window. Require mean cost below .01 on every source for persistent shift (641–1024) and transient return (769–1024). Report timer results and the individual costs even if the primary criterion fails. Reuse the pilot's interval function, resampling seeds within each contrast. Freeze these choices before collecting results.
